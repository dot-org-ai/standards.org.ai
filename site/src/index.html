Work

If it is a list of jobs, of the steps inside them, or of how you train for them, it is here.

7 standards, 3 held in part

  1. O*NET

    Every U.S. occupation, its tasks, its skills, its tools

    The whole standard. Feeds occupations · loaded db_30_0_text, the O*NET database version 30.0. Public domain, a U.S. Government work.

  2. APQC PCF

    Every process a business runs, named the same way across industries

    The whole standard. Feeds process · Process Classification Framework 7.4 Cross-Industry, plus the industry frameworks at 7.2.1 and 7.2.2. APQC membership or purchase; the framework file itself grants a perpetual royalty-free licence with attribution to APQC and IBM.

  3. BLS

    How many people hold each job, and what each pays

    Part of the standard. Feeds occupations and industries · Occupational Employment Statistics, May 2024; the projections matrix on 2022 NAICS and 2018 SOC. Public domain. One large series file is documented here and has not been decoded.

  4. Advance CTE

    Which course of study leads to which job

    The whole standard. Feeds skills and courses · Framework Crosswalk, September 2025. Free with attribution.

  5. ISCED

    UNESCO's levels of education and its fields of study

    The whole standard. Feeds education · ISCED 2011 for the levels, ISCED-F 2013 for the fields. UNESCO.

  6. CEDS

    The U.S. common vocabulary for school data

    Part of the standard. Feeds education · no version stated. A sample of its elements, not the set it publishes.

  7. CASE

    The format for publishing a set of learning standards

    Part of the standard. Feeds education · no version stated. The frameworks and the types, and none of the competencies underneath them.

Made and sold

If it is a way of naming what a business makes, buys or ships, it is here.

7 standards, 2 held in part

  1. NAICS

    How North America names its industries

    The whole standard. Feeds industries · NAICS 2022. Public domain.

  2. NAPCS

    The products those industries sell

    The whole standard. Feeds products · NAPCS 2022. Public domain.

  3. UNSPSC

    One code for anything a business buys

    The whole standard. Feeds products · no version stated; the download requires registration. The largest collection we hold.

  4. GS1

    What a product is, where it went, and the words for both

    The whole standard. Feeds products and logistics · product classification as of May 2025, with the November 2024 network release beside it; the event standard at 2.0.1; the web vocabulary at 1.17. GS1 General Specifications licence, free for members.

  5. eCl@ss

    The European classification for industrial and technical products

    Part of the standard. Feeds products · no version stated. The forty-nine segments at the top, and none of the forty thousand classes its own documentation says sit underneath them.

  6. ETIM

    The classification electrical wholesalers use

    Part of the standard. Feeds products · no version stated. Eighty example classes out of the several thousand it publishes.

  7. Schema.org

    The vocabulary the web uses to describe things to machines

    The whole standard. Feeds schema · no version stated. CC BY-SA 3.0.

Our own superset of Schema.org, schema.org.ai, is under Our standards.

Health

If a clinic, a pharmacy or an insurer has to write it on a form, it is here.

9 standards, 5 held in part

  1. ICD-10-CM

    Every diagnosis a U.S. clinician can write down

    The whole standard. Feeds health · fiscal year 2026, which runs 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026. Public domain.

  2. HCPCS Level II

    The codes for supplies and services a doctor bills for

    The whole standard. Feeds health · January 2026. Public domain, from CMS.

  3. NDC

    The FDA's directory of finished drug products

    Part of the standard. Feeds health and products · downloaded 5 December 2025. Ten thousand products out of the hundred and thirty-five thousand the directory holds. We took a sample.

  4. NPI provider taxonomy

    The code that says what kind of clinician someone is

    The whole standard. Feeds health and occupations · version 25.1, released 1 July 2025. Public use, maintained by NUCC.

  5. HL7 FHIR

    The shape of a health record moving between two systems

    The whole standard. Feeds health · R5, version 5.0.0. CC0. The resources and the datatypes.

  6. SNOMED CT

    Clinical terms

    Part of the standard. Feeds health · the top-level hierarchy only. The full set needs a licence from SNOMED International and we do not hold one.

  7. RxNorm

    Normalised drug names

    Part of the standard. Feeds health · the monthly release, October 2025. Free to use. The term types, and none of the drugs under them.

  8. LOINC

    The identifiers for lab results and measurements

    Part of the standard. Feeds health · 2.81, released 12 August 2025. Its axes and its categories, and none of its codes. Use is governed by the LOINC licence agreement.

  9. CPT

    The codes for medical procedures

    Part of the standard. Feeds health · category information only. The codes are the AMA's and need a licence we do not hold.

Two of these need a licence we do not hold: SNOMED CT and CPT. We hold the parts their publishers make public, and the rows say which.

Money and paperwork

If it identifies who is paying, or it is the message that carries the order, it is here.

9 standards, 4 held in part

  1. ISO 20022

    The message one bank sends another

    The whole standard. Feeds finance · no version stated. The message catalogue. ISO TC68 maintains it; SWIFT holds the registry.

  2. LEI

    The twenty-character code that says which company this is

    Part of the standard. Feeds business · no version stated. The registration authorities. GLEIF maintains it.

  3. ISIN

    The code for a security

    Part of the standard. Feeds finance · no version stated. The national numbering agencies. ANNA maintains it.

  4. MCC

    The four digits on a card payment that say what kind of shop it was

    The whole standard. Feeds finance and business · ISO 18245:2023. ISO TC68 and the card networks maintain it.

  5. BIC

    The code that identifies a bank

    Part of the standard. Feeds finance · ISO 9362:2014. The structure and its country codes, and not the register of banks itself. SWIFT maintains it.

  6. X12

    North America's electronic purchase orders and invoices

    The whole standard. Feeds logistics and products · no version stated. Its transaction sets, its segments and its elements.

  7. UN/EDIFACT

    The same idea, for the rest of the world

    Part of the standard. Feeds logistics · directory D.20B. Public domain under the UN terms of use. 195 of the 317 message types it publishes.

  8. EANCOM

    GS1's version of EDIFACT for consumer goods

    The whole standard. Feeds logistics and products · no version stated. GS1 maintains it.

  9. Peppol

    Europe's e-invoicing network, now used well beyond Europe

    The whole standard. Feeds logistics · no version stated. OpenPeppol maintains it.

The lists everyone quotes

If the other standards on this page cite it rather than compete with it, it is here.

5 standards, 1 held in part

  1. ISO 3166 · 4217 · 639

    Country codes, currency codes, language codes

    The whole standard. Feeds places, units and language · no edition stated. Some ISO standards are free and others must be bought.

  2. UN/LOCODE

    A code for every port, airport and inland place goods pass through

    The whole standard. Feeds places and logistics · our notes cite the loc241 release. Public domain under the UN terms of use.

  3. UN M49

    The UN's regions of the world

    Part of the standard. Feeds places · no version stated. The regions only, and none of the countries underneath them.

  4. IANA time zone database

    The zones, and the rules for when the clocks change

    The whole standard. Feeds units and places · taken from the vvo/tzdb mirror, downloaded 7 December 2025. Public domain.

  5. W3C

    The specifications the web is written to

    The whole standard. Feeds tech and schema · twenty-two of them, from JSON-LD and RDF to WCAG and ARIA. Per-specification versions are not stated. W3C Software and Document Licence, with MDN's licence cited for some tables.

Four of the twenty-two folders filed under W3C are not W3C's: DOM and HTML are WHATWG's, Dublin Core is DCMI's, FOAF is the FOAF project's. The folder name is a shelf label, not an attribution.

Government registers

If one government publishes it and everyone trading with that government has to use it, it is here.

6 standards, 4 held in part

  1. Census Bureau

    State and county codes, and the metro-area boundaries

    The whole standard. Feeds places · FIPS codes with the July 2023 metro-area delineation, downloaded 7 December 2025. Public domain.

  2. SEC

    The older industry list, still used on filings

    The whole standard. Feeds industries · the four-digit SIC list, downloaded 7 December 2025. Public domain.

  3. USITC

    The tariff schedule for goods entering the United States

    Part of the standard. Feeds products · downloaded 7 December 2025. Public domain. The chapters, and nothing beneath them.

  4. GSA

    The codes the federal government buys under

    Part of the standard. Feeds products and services · Product Service Code Manual, April 2025, with the supply classes beside it. Public domain. The classes, and almost nothing beneath them.

  5. SBA

    What counts as a small business

    Part of the standard. Feeds business · 13 CFR 121.201. Public domain. The business and contract types, and not the size thresholds by industry.

  6. USPTO

    The classes a trademark or a patent is filed under

    Part of the standard. Feeds law and products · Nice Classification 12th edition 2025, with Locarno, CPC and USPC beside it, downloaded 7 December 2025. Public domain. The Nice and Locarno classes are whole; the patent classifications are a handful of rows.

Four of these six are the beginning of a register rather than the register. Each row says which.

Our standards

If nobody outside this Foundation governs it, it is here. That is the whole test.

6 standards, written here

  1. AXP

    What an API has to publish so an agent can use it on first contact, with nobody reading the documentation

    Written by the Foundation. Draft 0.9.0, dated 23 August 2026. Published by apis.ax and verified by api.qa.

    axp.org.ai

  2. MDXLD

    MDX with $id, $type and $context in the frontmatter

    Written by the Foundation. No version number. A superset of the open MDX format, whose frontmatter is itself a superset of YAML-LD. $context is always https://schema.org.ai, with no version in it.

    mdx.org.ai/docsmdxld.org

  3. PRODUCT.md

    One Markdown file saying who a product is for, how it speaks and what it costs

    Written by the Foundation. Draft. A specification we write, not a standard we host.

    product.md

  4. TODO.md

    A task list a machine can also read, in a plain Markdown file

    Written by the Foundation. No version number. In use in this Foundation's own repos today.

    todo.md.org.ai

  5. schema.org.ai

    Our superset of Schema.org, extended for work machines do

    Written by the Foundation. No version number. Adds our own types on top of the ones Schema.org publishes. CC BY-SA 4.0; the vocabulary it extends is CC BY-SA 3.0.

    schema.org.ai

  6. AGENTS.md

    The frontmatter an AGENTS.md file carries so one file can compile to AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md and .cursorrules

    Written by the Foundation. No version number.

    agents.md.org.ai

AXP carries a version and a status in its own file: draft 0.9.0. The other five carry no version number yet. A standard of ours reaches 1.0 once at least two people outside the Foundation have built something that depends on it.
